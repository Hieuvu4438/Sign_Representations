## Summary of the paper

The paper evaluates frozen SignRep window embeddings under three readouts. Under a protocol locked before test access, ordered two-segment pooling (R1) and chamfer re-ranking (R2) beat SignRep's activity-weighted mean (R0). The gains are R1 +5.9 and R2 +9.4 R@1 on ASL Citizen test, +2.0 and +1.1 on WLASL2000, and +1.9 and +4.5 on MS-ASL. The paper adds exploratory diagnostics (time reversal, ASL-LEX error sharing, chamfer variants, signer distractors) and four negative method results: XWP, the CIP premise, the 32-frame student and set-to-vector distillation.

The confirmatory core is unusually careful for a workshop paper: locked protocol, logged test access, a reproduced baseline, a signer-cluster bootstrap and Holm correction. Most of the problems are in **framing and interpretation**, not in the headline numbers.

## 2. Claim audit (statements stronger than the evidence)

Ordered by severity.

### C-A. "Order-aware" is not established as the mechanism; the draft's own evidence points elsewhere

Affected text:
- Abstract: "a training-free readout that keeps the order of two temporal segments".
- Abstract, last sentence: "compared against the original encoder with an order-aware readout".
- Title: "Averaging hides".

Evidence against an order mechanism:
- The best readout, R2 chamfer, is **order-free**.
- One-directional q→g matching (68.7) beats symmetric chamfer.
- DTW, which enforces order, is worse than chamfer (51.6 vs 56.7).
- A temporal-position penalty adds +0.25 (§3.2).
- The gain is "not movement-specific" (§3.4).
- The draft itself concludes "phase coverage without temporal alignment".

For R1, the only order evidence is the half-swap collapse (bins2 0.533 → 0.226, gap_map.md; not in the draft and not in an artifact). That shows the halves differ, not that order is needed. gap_map.md says this itself: "Bin-shuffle collapse shows bins differ, not that order is semantically informative".

R1 also doubles dimensionality (1536-d vs 768-d) and restricts to the active span. There is no equal-dimension, order-free control.

**Fix:** call R1 a "two-segment" readout and R2 "window-set matching". Replace "order-aware" with "non-averaging" or "multi-vector" unless the order-free controls in §4 (E1) show a gap. Retitle, for example: "Averaging Hides What a Frozen Sign Encoder Knows: Readout Matters for Frozen SignRep Retrieval".

### C-B. §3.6 and Discussion bullet 2 / Abstract last sentence overstate "the readout changes conclusions about objectives"

§3.6 reports that **the ranking is the same under both readouts**. The gains under the averaged readout were also not significant: M0 − B0 CI [−0.05, +1.02] per R001; B0 − C0 [−0.76, +0.07] per eval_ex02_final.json. One seed was run, and the CIs are not saved (T1).

The draft's sentence "Part of what an objective appears to add under averaging is information the original encoder already had and a better readout recovers" is a causal reading of differences of about 0.1–0.4 points that are within noise.

**Fix:** report §3.6 as "no ranking change; differences within noise (1 seed)". Rest the recommendation on the size of the readout gap (+5.9/+9.4), which exceeds every encoder-side change measured, not on §3.6.

### C-C. Discussion bullet 3 contradicts §3.5

The draft says encoder objectives "moved retrieval by < 1 point regardless of the target". The 32-frame continuation moved it by **−5.8 / −7.7** points (EX11), and that run used 3000×12 steps, not "≈ 4k".

**Fix:** "Same-window-length continuation objectives moved R@1 by < 1 point; the 32-frame continuation degraded it by 6–8 points."

### C-D. The intro and abstract premise contradicts the project's own literature evidence

Draft sentences:
- "Self-supervised sign-language encoders are commonly compared by frozen dictionary retrieval" (Abstract).
- "Frozen evaluation is the standard way to compare SSL sign representations" (§1).

C006 and literature_synthesis §3 say the opposite. Label-free sign SSL papers (SHuBERT, SignDino, SSL-SLR) report trained heads (LoRA, linear, fine-tuning). Only SignRep (label-free) and SignSeek (gloss-supervised) report frozen retrieval.

**Fix:** "SignRep, the label-free sign encoder that reports frozen dictionary retrieval, evaluates it after averaging window embeddings; the same frozen protocol is used by SignSeek [P112]." Also add that frozen-evaluation conclusions are known to depend on the readout in general video SSL (V-JEPA attentive probe vs average pooling, P201). That turns a weak premise into an honest one.

### C-E. Phonology interpretation in the Abstract and §3.4 omits contrary evidence and is not chance-normalised

The abstract says errors "share handshape and location with the true sign far more often than movement". This compares raw rates (44/46 vs 27%) across features with different base rates.

- Lifts over chance are about 8.9× for handshape, 2.9× for location and 1.6× for movement. Even the lifts are not comparable across features with different cardinality and entropy.
- The same JSON (`val_perturb_loso.json`, "mp") contains a minimal-pair test. Movement-only minimal pairs are discriminated at **95.6%**, similar to location (94.0%). That argues against "averaging discards movement".
- The time-reversal diagnostic and the "who gains" analysis (not movement-specific) point the same way.
- ASL-LEX codes the initial morpheme only and has no orientation (disclosed), and no linguist has reviewed the analysis.

**Fix:** move the phonology sentence out of the abstract, report lift (or a chance-corrected agreement), report the minimal-pair accuracies, and describe these as "confusion statistics" rather than evidence of what is encoded.

### C-F. "Especially under signer shift (ASL Citizen)" (Discussion bullet 1) is an unsupported causal attribution

confirmatory_analysis.md shows that making the WLASL gallery signer-disjoint changes gains only slightly: bins2 +1.73 and chamfer +2.81, vs +1.51 / +1.89. The authors conclude that signer overlap "explains little" of the ASL Citizen–WLASL difference.

**Fix:** "largest on ASL Citizen; the cross-dataset difference is unexplained (not by duration, gallery size or signer overlap in our checks)."

### C-G. The abstract miscounts CIP as a "method attempt that did not help under matched controls"

No CIP objective was ever trained. Only the premise gates were run (D004: "CIP student pilot not run").

**Fix:** "a premise test for conditional cross-articulator forecasting found no usable increment (0/36 gates in three settings)."

### C-H. "Same storage class: one vector" (Discussion bullet 2)

R1 is 1536-d, twice R0. R2 needs storing up to 48×768 window vectors per gallery clip. The fp32 gallery window tensor needed 5.52 GiB (EX05b.log OOM line), against about 0.12 GB for R0 at 40,154×768 fp32.

**Fix:** state 2× dimension for R1, give the R2 storage and latency explicitly, and add an equal-dimension control (E1).

### C-I. Generality wording

- The title says "Frozen Sign Encoders" (plural) and "Self-Supervised Sign Representations". The confirmatory claim covers **one** encoder.
- Limitations calls SHuBERT a "replication", and §3.3 is a full results subsection. Per the user (D012), SHuBERT is reference-only and must not be a pillar.
- §3.3 also claims "the choice of internal feature matters as much as the readout for this encoder". That is a new SHuBERT-specific claim at floor-level accuracy (4–14 R@1).
- The residual-stream choice was made after the FFN-branch features failed (the EX06b amendment, written before the numbers, but still a post-hoc feature switch).
- The 6th val signer is degenerate (N6).

**Fix:** move SHuBERT to one appendix paragraph labelled "reference check, val only, not part of the claims". Remove the "matters as much as" sentence and the word "replication". Keep "one encoder" prominent in the title, abstract and limitations.

### C-J. Test-split hygiene statements

- The draft says "evaluated once". See N8: one logged re-run, non-locked variants also scored, and an EX10 partial extraction.
- MS-ASL is called a "fresh holdout". It is fresh for this project, but `method/research_lab` registered MS-ASL test metadata (MSEVALTEST001, MSFULLTEST001, D072). Those records say "no test-quality access". Apply the same repository-level disclosure as for ASL Citizen.

## 3. Novelty and positioning

### Strongest "already known" objection

> "That multi-vector matching beats a pooled clip vector on the same frozen features, and that time-ordered concatenation beats averaging, is established. TCA (Shao et al., WACV 2021, P206 Table 1f) reports frame-level chamfer ≫ video-level cosine on identical frozen descriptors (FIVR-5K DSVR 0.844 vs 0.609). LiFT (Bagad & Zisserman, P402 Table 2) shows average pooling of frozen per-frame features is near chance on chiral pairs while time-concatenation is strong (CLIP 53.5 vs 71.6 on SSv2), and even VideoMAE / V-JEPA gain from time-concatenation (80.3 → 85.7; 80.4 → 85.4). V-JEPA (P201 Table 3) shows frozen-evaluation conclusions swing with the probe (K400 56.7 average pool → 73.7 attentive). Most damaging, SignMatch (Wong et al., arXiv 2609.01886, P405) — by SignRep's own group — already aggregates SignRep-family window features with an order-aware LSTM for ASL Citizen dictionary retrieval and reports 49.95 → 67.12 R@1. So this paper re-applies a known readout to one encoder."

The draft has **no related-work section** and cites none of TCA, LiFT, SCALE, SignMatch or SignSeek. A reviewer who knows SignMatch will read that omission as overclaiming. This is a blocking issue.

### How to survive it

The paper's defensible contributions, all supported by the notes:

1. **A measured, locked-protocol estimate for sign retrieval specifically.** It uses the label-free sign encoder that reports frozen retrieval, a signer-disjoint benchmark, a single logged test access, and replication on two more datasets with a dataset-dependent size.
2. **A training-free, label-free contrast to SignMatch.** SignMatch's +17.2 R@1 mixes backbone fine-tuning, gloss and pseudo-label supervision, 32-frame windows and a learned LSTM, and it has no aggregator ablation (P405 critical analysis). This paper isolates the readout on the *released* windows: +9.4 R@1 with no training or labels beyond the gallery. Frame this as context, not as a ranking (C013): different windows, supervision and backbone.
3. **A sign-specific mechanism result that differs from LiFT's direction story.** In LiFT, gains come from direction. Here the gain is mostly phase coverage: q→g matching is best, alignment (DTW, position penalty) does not help, and the gain is not movement-specific. This is the more novel analytical point. Promote it once the order-free controls (E1) are run.
4. **Negative results** that rule out cheap encoder-side and label-free learned fixes at this budget.
5. **An evaluation recommendation**: report a readout family for frozen sign retrieval.

### Related-work sentences grounded only in the notes

Each source below is FULL_READ per its note. ViSiL, DnS and ColBERT-style late interaction appear in the notes only as DISCOVERED (TCA citations, search_log_E). **Read them before citing**, or cite only as "late-interaction / chamfer similarity [as used in TCA]".

- "Comparing videos by sets of frame- or segment-level descriptors rather than one pooled vector is established for near-duplicate video retrieval: with identical frozen descriptors, TCA reports that frame-level chamfer similarity outperforms video-level cosine by a large margin (FIVR-5K DSVR 0.844 vs 0.609) [Shao et al., WACV 2021]."
- "For frozen video features, average pooling discards temporal direction while time-ordered concatenation retains it, even for native video encoders such as VideoMAE and V-JEPA [Bagad & Zisserman, LiFT]; LiFT learns a compact time-aware vector without labels but evaluates with per-pair linear probes rather than retrieval."
- "SCALE trains a set-level transformer on frozen clip features without labels and improves k-NN accuracy for MAE backbones [Sameni et al., arXiv 2211.17042]; its objectives impose no order sensitivity."
- "In general video SSL the frozen evaluation outcome depends strongly on the readout (average pooling vs attentive probe) [V-JEPA, P201]."
- "In sign language, SignMatch aggregates sliding windows of a SignRep-initialised backbone with an LSTM and improves ASL Citizen dictionary retrieval substantially, but with gloss and pseudo-label supervision and a fine-tuned backbone, and without isolating the aggregator [Wong et al., arXiv 2609.01886]. SignSeek reports frozen ASL Citizen retrieval with gloss-supervised pose pretraining and attentive pooling [Asasi et al., arXiv 2609.03695]. We isolate the readout on the released, frozen SignRep windows without any training."
- "Alignment-based matching (DTW and its outlier-robust variant Drop-DTW [Dvornik et al., NeurIPS 2021]) enforces temporal order; in our setting it was worse than order-free set matching."
- "Frame-permutation sensitivity of a pose transformer was probed by SSL-SLR under linear evaluation (P103, Fig. 4); we are not aware of a reversal or readout analysis of a frozen RGB sign retrieval embedding (searches A–E, 2026-10-06; forward citations of SignRep not exhaustively enumerated)."

## 5. Overall recommendation

**Borderline: weak accept for a workshop if issues 1–5 below are fixed; weak reject for a Findings-style venue in the current form.**

Reasons to accept:
- The headline numbers are correct (one rounding error) and come from a locked, single-access protocol with a reproduced baseline.
- The effect is large on the signer-disjoint benchmark (11/11 signers for R2; 10/11 for R1), replicated in direction on two more datasets, and robust to signer-clustered resampling on WLASL for R1.
- Negative results are reported with matched controls.

Reasons it falls short of Findings:
1. The draft has no related work. The SignMatch, TCA and LiFT objection is unanswered, and the core idea is known in general video retrieval.
2. The mechanism language ("order-aware", "averaging discards movement") is contradicted by the paper's own diagnostics.
3. The main practical implication (§3.6, "readout changes conclusions about objectives") is not supported by the data.
4. One encoder.
5. Several text-level contradictions: the abstract premise vs C006, Discussion bullet 3 vs EX11, CIP described as a trained objective.

These are mostly fixable with rewriting, plus E1–E5. None needs new test-set access.
