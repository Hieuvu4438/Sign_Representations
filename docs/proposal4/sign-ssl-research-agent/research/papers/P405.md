## Mechanism

- Gradient path: Stage 1 updates the backbone, projection and prototypes. Stage 2 trains only the LSTM aggregator over frozen window features.
- The ordered aggregator (LSTM over sliding windows) is order-aware by construction. Its use is motivated by citation-form signs being longer ("dictionary videos are windowed and aggregated rather than matched at a fixed length", §3.1), not by direction sensitivity.
- Soft prototype targets make visually similar classes close (App. C Fig. 8).

## Critical analysis

- Strengths: large-scale, real sign corpora. Uses exactly SignRep as RGB backbone. Reports ASL Citizen retrieval with the SignRep baseline.
- Author-stated limitations (App. D): geometry not yet characterised by handshape/location/movement/orientation. Citation vs co-articulated domain gap.
- INFERENCE: no time-reversal, order, or shuffle diagnostic (python keyword scan for revers/arrow/order/shuffle/permut/chiral: no hits). No LSTM-vs-mean ablation. The paper therefore cannot say whether its ASL Citizen gain needs temporal order.
- INFERENCE: "SignRep (SSL)" baseline pooling over windows is not specified in the text read (UNKNOWN). Their 49.95 R@1 is on the official ASL Citizen protocol (test). Our numbers are val, leave-one-signer-out, so not comparable.
- Confound: ASLDict comes from signasl.org. Overlap of its signers/videos with ASL Citizen is not discussed in text read (UNKNOWN). Phase 2 vocabulary overlap with ASL Citizen glosses is likely, given ASL dictionaries, so "unseen signs" is ambiguous.

## Overlap with H1/H2

- H1. What SignMatch already does:
  - frozen SignRep-family window features (32-frame, stride 16, over the active signing interval) aggregated by an **ordered** sequence model into a single retrieval embedding;
  - evaluation on ASL Citizen dictionary retrieval;
  - +17.17 R@1 over the SignRep baseline. This is a *supervised*, fine-tuned backbone result, not label-free.
- What SignMatch does not do:
  - supervision: it is not label-free. The aggregator is trained with dictionary labels and prototypes, and the backbone is fine-tuned on gloss-labelled data;
  - order: no evidence that order or direction matters, and no reversal metric;
  - aggregator ablation: none (mean vs LSTM vs ordered bins);
  - leave-one-signer-out protocol: not used;
  - phonological confusion analysis: none, explicitly left to future work.
- Threat level for H1: HIGH on framing ("time-aware aggregation of SignRep windows for dictionary retrieval" is already done, with supervision, by the SignRep authors). MEDIUM on method (label-free objective is still open). Any H1 paper must cite it and position H1 as the label-free counterpart, plus the order-sensitivity analysis.
- H2: no overlap (Stage 1 fine-tunes the backbone with supervised prototypes, not latent prediction).
- Separating experiment: on the same frozen SignRep windows and the same ASL Citizen protocol, compare (i) weighted mean, (ii) ordered 2-bin, (iii) H1 label-free encoder, (iv) a supervised LSTM aggregator trained on ASL Citizen *train* dictionary labels (SignMatch-style Stage-2 analogue, clearly labelled as supervised), and (v) the same aggregators on time-reversed input. If (iii) ≥ (iv) without labels, that is the defensible claim. If (iv) ≫ (iii), H1's value reduces to the label-free setting only.
