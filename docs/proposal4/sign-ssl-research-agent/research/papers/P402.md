## Mechanism

- Gradient path: only the LiFT encoder/decoder. DINOv2 is frozen. Information is forced through a 2-vector bottleneck in which time enters only linearly via (t/T)·z_d, so the "arc of change" must be stored in z_d. A reversed video needs approximately z_s' ≈ z_s + z_d and z_d' ≈ −z_d. Direction is therefore represented as a sign flip of z_d. INFERENCE from Eq. 4, not shown explicitly by the authors beyond the t-SNE in Fig. 3b.
- Authors' rationale: the Perceptual Straightening hypothesis (Hénaff et al.). Smooth feature trajectories can be mapped to lines.
- Condition: per-frame base features must encode the changing quantity. C.4 shows they fail for horizontal position: in synthetic disc sequences, DINOv2 "Time Variance" is about 25% higher for vertical than horizontal motion.
- Novelty claimed: (1) the chiral action recognition task/benchmark (CiA); (2) LiFT; (3) complementarity with video models.

## Critical analysis

- Strengths: clean, cheap, label-free recipe on frozen features. Explicit time-sensitivity benchmark with controls (single-frame, two-frame, pooling type: Fig. 5, 9, 10). Seeds reported. Honest failure analysis (C.4).
- Author-stated limitations: per-frame CLS loses spatial detail, so horizontal translation is poorly encoded (§6, C.4). Chirality is defined on naturally opposite actions; they "do not artificially reverse the arrow of time" (§3, §5).
- INFERENCE: the line model assumes a monotone trajectory (one direction of change). Signs with multi-segment movement (hold–move–hold–move, repeated or circular movement) are not lines in feature space. A single z_d cannot represent a reversal mid-sign or a cyclic movement. Their own data ablation hints at this: K400 videos with little change give little signal (C.2).
- INFERENCE: evaluation is a supervised linear probe per binary chiral pair. It is not nearest-neighbour retrieval and not open-vocabulary. A z_d sign flip is linearly separable, but under cosine retrieval it moves the descriptor by roughly ‖z_d‖ relative to ‖z_s‖. Whether reversal is visible to kNN depends on that norm ratio, which the paper does not report.
- Confound: the CiA groups come from datasets with object-centric visual change. Chiral pairs were mined with an LLM and checked manually. Training (K400) and evaluation (SSv2/EPIC/Charades) are different datasets, which is a plus.
- Horizontal-motion weakness is directly relevant to sign language. Much of sign movement is lateral, so a frame-level image backbone may be the wrong substrate. Our backbone is a video Hiera window encoder, not a per-frame image model.

## Overlap with H1/H2

- H1. What LiFT already does:
  - label-free temporal encoder on frozen features → one time-aware vector;
  - trajectory objective (reconstruct the feature trajectory through a linear latent): this is H1's "trajectory" variant almost exactly;
  - an explicit time-direction evaluation;
  - the observation that mean pooling destroys direction while concatenation keeps it (Table 2, Fig. 5c), which is the same phenomenon as our weighted-mean reversal cosine 0.959 and 2-bin gain.
- What LiFT does not do:
  - domain: no sign language;
  - input type: per-frame image CLS (DINOv2), not windows from a video backbone (SignRep 16-frame Hiera windows), and no hand-activity weighting or active-span cropping;
  - evaluation regime: linear probe on binary chiral pairs, no kNN or retrieval experiment (retrieval appears only as a claim that LiFT combined with video models is "useful for classification, retrieval and search", §4.2; verified by a python keyword scan), no signer-independent split, no open-vocabulary gallery;
  - reversal measurement: no artificial time reversal and no clip-vs-reversed cosine; time sensitivity is measured only through naturally opposite pairs;
  - objective coverage: no masked-feature-prediction variant, and no handling of non-monotone or multi-segment trajectories.
- H2: no overlap (backbone frozen). §6 suggests combining with masked modelling / autoregression as future work.
- Remaining delta for H1 vs LiFT: (a) window-level video features rather than frame-level image features; (b) non-linear, multi-segment trajectories (movement–hold structure); (c) retrieval-oriented evaluation (R@k, signer-independent) plus an explicit reversal test; (d) sign phonology confusion analysis (movement vs handshape/location). (a) and (c) are protocol deltas, not method deltas. (b) is the only mechanism-level delta, and it is unproven.
- Single best separating experiment: run LiFT unchanged on SignRep window sequences (z_s ⊕ z_d) as the baseline for H1. Report ASL Citizen leave-signer-out R@1, the reversal cosine, and movement-only-differing pair accuracy. Also report an ordered 2-bin readout (no training) as the floor. H1 is only interesting if it beats LiFT-on-SignRep on R@1 at similar reversal sensitivity.
