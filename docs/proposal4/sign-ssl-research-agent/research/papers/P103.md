## Mechanism

- Augmentation: random temporal permutation of the first ≈N/3 and last ≈N/4 frames (transition/epenthesis parts) while preserving the middle (§5.2). Contrastive/BYOL-like invariance then makes the representation ignore boundary frames and depend on the central part.
- Algorithm 1 finds the boundaries by progressively permuting frames and measuring linear-eval accuracy (Fig. 4): accuracy stable until ~N/3 (start) and ~N/4 (end), "collapsing completely when k_s=N (all frames permuted)" (§5.2).
- Collapse prevention: predictor + stop-gradient + LayerNorm (§7 ablation, Fig. 8).

## Critical analysis

- Strengths: simple negative-free objective; explicit test of frame-order importance; transfer across sign languages (Table 2).
- Author-stated limitations (§8): performance has room to improve on large-scale datasets; boundary importance "currently determined empirically"; future non-empirical method; continuous SLR not covered.
- INFERENCE: augmentation hyperparameters tuned by labelled linear-eval accuracy → not purely SSL; risk of test/selection leakage unclear (which split was used for Algorithm 1 not stated in what I read).
- INFERENCE: Table 6 compares fine-tuned SSL-SLR with frozen SignRep retrieval and SignCLIP fine-tuned numbers in one column — a concrete instance of cross-setting comparison in recent literature.
- Temporal modelling: whole-sign (≤64 frame) transformer with positional encoding; invariance learned to boundary-frame order, sensitivity kept for central frames — closest prior art to "order-aware whole-sign SSL" but in pose modality.
- Nuisance: pose input chosen for "insensitivity to visual factors" (§6.2); no signer-shift test.
- Pose noise: not addressed (no confidence handling described).

## Liên hệ project

- H-seed-A: shows frame-order sensitivity is measurable via permutation; provides a ready diagnostic (permute first/last/central portions and measure frozen retrieval).
- Threat: "temporal permutation augmentation" for sign SSL is already published; novelty must come from elsewhere (RGB, frozen retrieval, objective).
- Cheap experiment: apply SSL-SLR's permutation protocol to frozen SignRep 16-frame windows + weighted pooling on ASL Citizen retrieval; expected insensitivity across windows (INFERENCE) would quantify the order gap.
